Mini Golf

Mini golf with 20 holes. Drag back from anywhere on the course and let go, like a slingshot: the further you drag, the harder the putt. There are walls, bumpers, sand, water, sliding gates and windmills, and a scorecard at the end.

It builds on Pool: the same game loop, rolling with friction and bouncing. The new ideas are keeping the holes as data, one bounce function for every kind of wall, things that move, and controls that work with a finger as well as a mouse.

Step 3: Everything is a line

The ball bounces off the outline, thin walls and blocks. Instead of writing a bounce for each, we turn them all into line segments, straight lines from one point to another. The outline is a segment from each corner to the next, and a block is its four edges:

@@fn:segment@@

@@fn:rectSegments@@

@@fn:buildCourse@@
  • (i + 1) % level.course.length is the next corner, and for the last corner it is 0, the first one again.
  • half is half a wall's thickness. Walls are drawn 8 pixels thick, so the ball must bounce 4 pixels before the line itself.
  • level.walls || [] means "the walls, or an empty list if the hole has none", so a hole only lists what it has.
  • ...rectSegments(rect, 0): the three dots put the four edges into the list one by one, instead of one list inside the other.

Step 4: Bouncing off a line

This is the heart of the game. It works for any line, at any angle:

@@fn:bounceOffSegment@@
  1. The closest point. t says how far along the line the ball is, from 0 at the start to 1 at the end. The math is a dot product: how much of the way from the start to the ball goes the same way as the line. Keeping t between 0 and 1 makes the ball hit the end of a wall when it comes from the side, so corners work too.
  2. Too close? If the ball's center is nearer than its radius (plus half the wall), it is touching.
  3. Push it out along nx, ny, the direction from the wall to the ball, until it just touches.
  4. Bounce. into is how fast the ball moves towards the wall. Taking it away once stops the ball at the wall; taking it away 1 + WALL_BOUNCE times sends it back with 75% of that speed. The speed along the wall stays as it is, so the ball bounces off at the same angle it came in, like light in a mirror.

velocityAt is a function that says how fast the wall itself moves at a point. For a normal wall it is still(), which is always 0. We need it for the moving things in step 7: a block that moves into the ball should push it.

Bumpers are circles, and work the same way, with a closest point that is simply the bumper's middle. They give back a little more than they get: BUMPER_BOUNCE is 1.15.

Step 5: Rolling

One step of the ball, 1/60 of a second:

@@fn:rollStep@@
  • Small steps. At full power the ball moves 16 pixels a step, which is more than its own size. It could jump right through a thin wall between two steps. So it moves in 4 small steps of at most 4 pixels, and checks every wall after each one.
  • Friction. As in Pool, the ball keeps a part of its speed each step, 98.5% on grass and 92% in sand. Taking away a little extra as well (0.02) makes it stop in the end, instead of creeping on and on at a slower and slower speed.
  • Too fast for the hole. The ball only drops in if it is slower than SINK_SPEED. A hard putt rolls straight over the hole, just like in real golf. And a ball that stops right at the edge of the hole drops in.
  • rollStep only uses its arguments, nothing else in the game. That makes it easy to test: a test page can try thousands of putts on a copy of the ball, to check that every hole can be played. That is how the pars in LEVELS were checked.

Step 7: Things that move

A mover slides to and fro. Math.sin goes smoothly from -1 to 1 and back, so multiplying it by range gives an offset that goes from -range to range, slowing down at the ends like a swing:

@@fn:moverOffset@@

@@fn:moverVelocity@@

How fast the mover goes is how fast its offset changes, and the change of sin is cos. The mover is fastest in the middle, where cos is 1, and stands still at the ends, where it is 0. In rollStep, the mover's four edges are made new every small step, and its speed goes to bounceOffSegment, so a moving block knocks the ball away.

A windmill blade is a line through the middle, turned by time * speed:

@@fn:bladeSegments@@

@@fn:bladeVelocity@@

A point on a turning blade moves at a right angle to the blade, and faster the further out it is, just like the end of a clock hand moves more than the middle. Turning the arrow from the middle to the point a quarter turn, and multiplying it by the speed, gives exactly that.

Movers and windmills move all the time, also while you aim. So the ball is checked every step, even when it lies still: if a blade hits it, it starts to roll.

Step 8: Drag to putt, with a mouse or a finger

Pool used mouse events. Pointer events are the same for a mouse, a finger and a pen, so one set of code works on a computer and on a phone:

canvas.addEventListener("pointerdown", function (event) {
    if (screen !== "playing") {
        goOn();
        return;
    }
    if (!ballStill()) {
        return;   // wait until the ball has stopped
    }
    // Keep getting the moves even when the finger leaves the canvas.
    try {
        canvas.setPointerCapture(event.pointerId);
    } catch (error) {
        // Some browsers say no; dragging still works inside the canvas.
    }
    dragging = true;
    dragStart = canvasPosition(event);
    dragNow = dragStart;
});

canvas.addEventListener("pointermove", function (event) {
    if (dragging) {
        dragNow = canvasPosition(event);
    }
});

canvas.addEventListener("pointerup", function () {
    if (dragging && screen === "playing" && ballStill()) {
        putt();
    }
    dragging = false;
});

The putt goes the opposite way of the drag, like a slingshot, and the length of the drag is the power:

@@fn:puttFromDrag@@

You can start the drag anywhere, not only on the ball. On a phone the course is small, and a finger would hide the ball anyway.

setPointerCapture sends all the moves to the canvas until the finger is lifted, even when it slides outside. Without it, a long drag that leaves the canvas would never putt.

The game doesn't need a keyboard at all: a click or a tap starts it and goes on to the next hole. That is why it can be installed as an app on a phone, which Pool can't, since it needs Enter to start.

Step 9: The score

Every hole has a par: how many strokes a good player needs. Golf names a score by how far it is from par:

@@fn:scoreName@@

names[strokesTaken - par] looks up the difference, so 2 strokes on a par 3 is names[-1], "Birdie!". If the name isn't in the object, the lookup gives undefined, and || picks the text after it.

At the end, a scorecard shows all 20 holes, and the lowest total is saved with localStorage, as in the Bubble level.

Step 10: Test it

  • Putt as hard as you can into each kind of wall, also into the corners. The ball should never go through.
  • Putt hard straight over the hole. It should roll over it. Putt softly and it should drop in.
  • Putt into the water on hole 6. The ball should come back, with one more stroke.
  • Stop the ball in the path of the gate on hole 9. The gate should push it.
  • Open the game on a phone, and drag with a finger. The page should not scroll.
  • Play all 20 holes, and check the scorecard adds up.
