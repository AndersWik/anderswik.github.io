Responsive images

A phone doesn't need the same big image as a large screen. With srcset, you give the browser several sizes of the image, and it chooses.

Different sizes

<img src="cat-800.jpg"
     srcset="cat-400.jpg 400w, cat-800.jpg 800w, cat-1600.jpg 1600w"
     sizes="(max-width: 600px) 100vw, 50vw"
     alt="A cat" width="800" height="533">
  • srcset lists the files and their widths in pixels, with w.
  • sizes tells how wide the image is shown: the whole screen (100vw) on screens up to 600px wide, else half of it.
  • The browser picks the smallest file that is sharp enough, also on high-resolution screens.
  • src is used by old browsers.

Sharp on high-resolution screens

For an image that is always shown at the same size, like a logo, give a version with twice the pixels:

<img src="logo.png" srcset="logo.png 1x, logo@2x.png 2x" alt="Logo" width="200" height="50">

Different images or formats

<picture> chooses between <source> elements. The browser uses the first one it can, and else the <img>:

<picture>
  <source srcset="cat.avif" type="image/avif">
  <source srcset="cat.webp" type="image/webp">
  <img src="cat.jpg" alt="A cat" width="800" height="533">
</picture>

With media instead of type, you can show another crop of the image on small screens.
