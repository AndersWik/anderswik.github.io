Semantic elements

Semantic elements say what their content is. A <div> could be anything, but a <nav> is always a menu. Screen readers use them to jump to the main content or the menu, and search engines use them to understand the page.

<body>
  <header>
    <a href="/">My website</a>
    <nav>
      <a href="/">Home</a>
      <a href="/recipes/">Recipes</a>
    </nav>
  </header>

  <main>
    <article>
      <h1>Chocolate cake</h1>
      <section>
        <h2>Ingredients</h2>
        ...
      </section>
      <section>
        <h2>How to bake it</h2>
        ...
      </section>
    </article>

    <aside>
      <h2>More cakes</h2>
      ...
    </aside>
  </main>

  <footer>
    <p>© 2026 My website</p>
  </footer>
</body>

The elements

  • <header>, the top of the page or of an article: logo, title and often the menu.
  • <nav>, a menu of links to other pages or parts of the page.
  • <main>, the main content. Only one per page.
  • <article>, content that makes sense on its own: a blog post, a news story, a product.
  • <section>, a part of a page or article, usually with a heading.
  • <aside>, content beside the main content: related links, ads, facts.
  • <footer>, the bottom of the page or of an article: contact, copyright.

These elements don't change how the page looks. The layout is made with CSS, for example with flexbox or grid.
