Entities and symbols

Some characters have a special meaning in HTML. < starts a tag, so to show a < on the page, write a character reference, also called an entity:

<p>5 &lt; 10 and 10 &gt; 5</p>
<p>Fish &amp; chips</p>

Common entities

  • &lt; is < and &gt; is >
  • &amp; is &
  • &quot; is " (in attribute values)
  • &nbsp; is a space where the line can't break, as in 10&nbsp;kr
  • &copy; is ©, &euro; is € and &hellip; is …

Numbers

Every character can also be written with its number in Unicode, in decimal or in hex:

<p>&#169; and &#xA9; are both ©</p>
<p>&#x1F600; is 😀</p>

Or just type it

When the page is saved as UTF-8, with <meta charset="utf-8">, you can type ©, €, å and emojis directly in the HTML. Only <, > and & must always be written as entities. See Charset.

The ASCII table lists the number and the entity for the first 256 characters.
