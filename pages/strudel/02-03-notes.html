Notes and scales

note plays melodies. It takes a pattern of notes, and sound chooses the instrument.

Notes as letters

Write notes with the letters a to g. Add # for sharp or b for flat.

note("c e g b").sound("piano")
note("db eb gb ab bb").sound("piano")
note("c# d# f# g# a#").sound("piano")

A number after the letter is the octave. A higher number is a higher note.

note("c2 e3 g4 b5").sound("piano")

Notes as numbers

Notes can also be numbers, the same numbers as in MIDI. 60 is middle C, and each step up is a half tone.

note("48 52 55 59").sound("piano")

Chords

A comma plays notes at the same time, so a comma inside brackets is a chord.

note("48 67 63 [62, 58]").sound("piano")

Instruments

sound chooses the instrument, and it is a pattern too. The gm_ sounds are General MIDI instruments.

note("<36 34 41 39>").sound("gm_acoustic_bass")
note("60 <63 62 65 63>").sound("gm_xylophone")

A comma plays two instruments at the same time:

note("48 67 63 [62, 58]").sound("piano, gm_electric_guitar_muted")

There are also simple synthesizer sounds: sine, square, triangle and sawtooth.

note("c2 c3 c2 c3").sound("sawtooth")

Scales

With n and scale, the numbers are steps in a scale instead of notes. 0 is the first note of the scale, 1 the second, and so on. It is hard to play a wrong note this way.

n("0 2 4 6").scale("C:minor").sound("piano")

The scale is written as root note, colon, and the name of the scale, for example C:major, A:minor or D:mixolydian. The scale can be a pattern, so it can change over time:

setcpm(60)
n("<0 -3>, 2 4 <[6,8] [7,9]>").scale("<C:major D:mixolydian>/4").sound("piano")

A bassline

note("<[c2 c3]*4 [bb1 bb2]*4 [f2 f3]*4 [eb2 eb3]*4>")
  .sound("gm_synth_bass_1")
  .lpf(800)

Each cycle plays one of the four parts, and each part plays the same note in two octaves, four times. lpf is a filter that makes the sound darker, see Effects.
