About the codes

ISO 3166-1 is the international standard for country codes. Every country, and some territories like Greenland and Puerto Rico, gets three codes:

  • Alpha-2, two letters, like SE for Sweden. The most used code: in language tags like sv-SE, in postal addresses and for most country domains.
  • Alpha-3, three letters, like SWE. Easier to recognise than two letters.
  • Numeric, three digits, like 752. The same for all languages and alphabets. The United Nations uses these for statistics.

Good to know

  • The country domains mostly use the alpha-2 code, but not always: the United Kingdom is GB, and its domain is .uk.
  • Kosovo is not in ISO 3166-1, but XK and XKX are often used, for example by the European Union.
  • Codes can change when a country changes its name. Czechia, North Macedonia, Eswatini and Türkiye kept their codes, but some old countries, like Yugoslavia (YU), were removed.
  • In the language codes, letters are small, and in the country codes they are capitals: sv is Swedish, SE is Sweden. Together they make a locale, like sv_SE.

Source: the ISO 3166-1 data in the Debian iso-codes package.
